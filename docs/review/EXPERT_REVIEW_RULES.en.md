## I. Purpose of Expert Review

Expert review examines the principles' conceptual, factual, theoretical, legal, ethical, technical, governance, and evaluation quality. Experts need not agree with the project's position to participate. Criticism, opposition, and challenges to fundamental assumptions are valid contributions.

Expert status confers no final interpretive authority and does not automatically place opinions above workers' experience. Judgments concerning actual work processes, understandable language, and the bearing of risks should be compared with worker review through separate tracks.

---

## II. Eligible Professional Backgrounds

Relevant backgrounds include, but are not limited to:

- Artificial intelligence, machine learning, data science, and software engineering;
- Technology ethics, algorithmic governance, and data protection;
- Labor relations, trade union studies, and collective bargaining;
- Labor law, social security, and public policy;
- Sociology, political economy, labor history, and organizational studies;
- Occupational health, workplace safety, human factors, and industrial engineering;
- Factory production, logistics, construction, the platform economy, services, and care work;
- Anti-discrimination, accessibility, social services, and worker support practice;
- Other fields offering verifiable knowledge or long-term practical experience relevant to the provisions under review.

Eligibility is based on relevant, reasonably verifiable experience, not educational level, institutional prestige, professional title, or support for the project. Practitioners may apply as experts.

## III. Participation Methods

### 3.1 Ordinary Comments from an Expert Perspective

Anyone may publicly submit comments from a self-described professional perspective. Without verification, the register labels them “unverified public comments”; they must not count toward formal expert numbers.

### 3.2 Formal Expert Review

Applicants for formal expert review should:

1. Provide at least one nonpublic contact method;
2. Describe their professional field, relevant experience, and limits of applicability of their judgment;
3. Provide one low-risk, verifiable item of professional background information;
4. Declare potential conflicts of interest;
5. Confirm that they have read these rules, publication authorization choices, and the privacy notice.

See [REVIEWER_RECRUITMENT_AND_SELECTION.en.md](REVIEWER_RECRUITMENT_AND_SELECTION.en.md) for application and selection rules, and use [EXPERT_REVIEW_APPLICATION_FORM.en.md](EXPERT_REVIEW_APPLICATION_FORM.en.md) for formal private applications. Verification methods are in [IDENTITY_VERIFICATION_POLICY.en.md](IDENTITY_VERIFICATION_POLICY.en.md). Do not submit contact details or supporting materials in public Issues, Discussions, or Pull Requests. A public “expert review participation interest” Issue only registers interest; it is not a formal application.

### 3.3 Targeted Invitations and Voluntary Applications

Targeted invitations do not imply advance endorsement of invitees' views. Voluntary applicants and invited participants follow the same verification, conflict-of-interest, and feedback rules. Selection must not depend on agreement with the current draft. Invitations must use the [expert invitation template](EXPERT_INVITATION_TEMPLATE.en.md) to specify scope, timing, compensation, publication arrangements, and withdrawal rights. During the current preparation stage, the project lead must approve each prospective invitation individually. Significant concerns or conflicts require review by an unconflicted person; the project lead sends invitations and takes responsibility.

## IV. Review Package

Each round should provide reviewers with:

- The frozen principles version, commit, and file hash;
- The round's scope, deadline, and expected feedback date;
- Versions of these rules and the review form;
- A summary of the preceding round's main disputes and revisions;
- Links to applicable terminology explanations, evaluation questions, and scoring rules;
- Public and nonpublic submission channels;
- Privacy, safety, licensing, and withdrawal information.

Reviewers may review only provisions relevant to their expertise, but should identify the unreviewed scope. Silence must not be interpreted as approval.

---

## V. Review Scope

Experts should focus on whether:

- Concepts are clear and terminology is used correctly;
- Provisions are consistent and conflict-resolution rules are adequate;
- Facts, research, and legal grounds are accurate and specify applicable periods and regions;
- Principles can be translated into clear AI behavior requirements;
- Principles can become repeatable evaluation criteria that distinguish performance;
- Wording is impossible to implement or verify, or easy to circumvent;
- Important labor relations, industries, or groups are omitted;
- Enterprises, platforms, governments, managers, or the project itself could turn provisions against intended beneficiaries;
- Workers' choices and agency are restricted in the name of protecting them;
- Privacy, safety, discrimination, retaliation, or other serious risks exist.

## VI. Structured Assessment

Structured scores locate problems. They are not used to rank experts, take simple averages, or combine scores with worker scores.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Does not meet the dimension at all, or clearly conflicts with its objective |
| 1 | Major defects requiring substantive revision |
| 2 | Partially meets the dimension; significant revision still needed |
| 3 | Largely meets the dimension; only minor revisions or additions needed |
| 4 | Fully meets the dimension; no substantive problems identified |
| N/A | Not applicable or outside my scope of judgment; reason required |

Recommended dimensions for each provision:

1. Conceptual clarity;
2. Internal consistency;
3. Factual, evidentiary, and legal accuracy;
4. Implementability;
5. Evaluability;
6. Coverage of labor relations and groups;
7. Rights, safety, and protection against misuse against intended beneficiaries.

Every score must include a brief reason. When samples are insufficient, present only original distributions and disagreements; do not use averages to manufacture stable conclusions.

A blank means unanswered; N/A means not applicable or outside the scope of judgment. Neither may be treated as 0. Summaries should report valid score counts, blank counts, N/A counts, original distributions, and medians for each provision and dimension. Scores trigger further scrutiny only; they do not automatically determine adoption, rejection, or maturity and must not be combined with worker scores.

## VII. Conclusions for Individual Provisions

For each principle, reviewers may choose:

- Recommend retaining;
- Recommend revising;
- Recommend splitting;
- Recommend merging;
- Recommend deleting;
- Recommend adding a principle;
- Unable to judge for now;
- Major dispute requiring further discussion.

After selecting a conclusion, explain the reasons, affected groups, proposed wording, and possible new risks.

---

## VIII. Evidence and Citations

- Distinguish verified facts, professional judgments, value judgments, and hypotheses;
- Legal opinions should state jurisdiction, time, and whether further confirmation by a local professional is needed;
- Provide authors, titles, years, and public links where possible;
- Do not fabricate papers, laws, data, institutions, cases, or collaborations;
- When identifying factual errors in the draft, provide grounds that can be checked;
- Insufficient evidence does not mean a problem does not exist; it may be marked “requires further investigation”;
- Workers may provide true cases they personally experienced, directly know about, or have reasonable factual grounds for, and choose real-name, pseudonymous, anonymous, or internal-only handling. Name, affiliation, contact details, original evidence, and case text require separate authorizations; material awaiting verification must not be presented as confirmed fact.

Formal review does not establish a legal, medical, or other professional service relationship. For high-risk actions, participants should be advised to seek qualified local professional assistance.

---

## IX. Conflicts of Interest and Independence

Under [GOVERNANCE_AND_ROLES.en.md](GOVERNANCE_AND_ROLES.en.md), reviewers should disclose drafting or co-authorship, employment, management, ownership, funding, contracts, commercial interests, ongoing disputes, direct relationships with submitters or decision-makers, and other relevant facts.

A conflict does not necessarily invalidate comments. A coordinator or temporary reviewer uninvolved in the relationship classifies it under public rules; the reviewer's self-classification is not final. The project should retain substantive comments with necessary contextual labels, but those with major conflicts must not participate in final decisions, identity verification, or reconsideration of the same matter. Having publicly expressed strong views is not itself a conflict.

---

## X. Publication, Attribution, and Licensing

The following choices are separate; consent must not be bundled:

- Whether to publish a name or public display name;
- Whether to publish professional background;
- Whether to publish affiliation;
- Whether to publish the complete substantive review;
- Whether to publish only an anonymized summary confirmed by the contributor;
- Whether to permit internal project processing only.

Public contributions are intended to use the repository content license, CC BY-SA 4.0. Nonpublic content without consent to that license may be used only as internal leads, not published verbatim or incorporated into public text.

---

## XI. Conduct, Withdrawal, and Feedback

- Respect other participants; do not harass, disparage, or investigate identities because of differing positions;
- Do not publish unauthorized private communications or internal materials;
- Unpublished content may be corrected, supplemented, or withdrawn before the round closes;
- Published, licensed content may already have been copied, forked, or lawfully reused. The project cannot guarantee complete retrieval, but will handle identification risks under privacy and safety rules;
- Formal reviews receive receipts, reasoned feedback, and one opportunity for procedural reconsideration under [REVIEW_RESPONSE_POLICY.en.md](REVIEW_RESPONSE_POLICY.en.md).

Fabricated identities, impersonation, repeated manipulation of participant counts, harassment, or knowingly submitting harmful materials may be marked invalid. Disagreement, sharp criticism, or low scores alone are not grounds for invalidation.
