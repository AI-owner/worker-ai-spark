# Worker Review Rules for the Basic Principles for Workers' AI

> **Chinese version:** [阅读中文版](WORKER_REVIEW_RULES.md)

> Status: Public discussion draft<br>
> Version: v0.2-draft<br>
> Formal review is not yet open. Participation does not require programming skills, familiarity with GitHub, formal educational qualifications, or knowledge of theoretical terminology.

---

## I. The Purpose of Worker Review

Worker review assesses whether the principles are understandable, reflect actual work processes, help workers, overlook important issues, or could be turned against workers by managers or other actors with power.

Workers are not objects to be studied, represented, or educated. Individuals may review only provisions relevant to their own experience and may express views that differ from the project's current draft, experts, or other workers.

---

## II. Who Can Participate

For these rules, workers include people who currently rely, or previously relied, primarily on their labor for their livelihood, such as:

- Factory workers, frontline engineers, drivers, delivery workers, and shop assistants;
- Healthcare, care, education, cleaning, and public service workers;
- Platform workers, agency workers, temporary workers, migrant workers, and people in flexible employment;
- Self-employed people with substantial economic dependence on a single platform or contracting party;
- People who are unemployed, retired, away from work due to injury, or changing occupations, but have relevant work experience;
- Others with direct experience of work processes.

People with frontline management responsibilities or who also run a small business are not automatically excluded, but should state which experience primarily informs their submission. Formal reviewers select one primary role for each submission to avoid double counting.

At this stage, the project does not actively recruit anyone under 18 for formal review requiring identity verification, nor collect minors' identity documents. Minors may submit ordinary public comments containing no identifying information when appropriate supervision and safety conditions are in place; these will not count as formal reviews until the project establishes dedicated protection rules.

---

## III. Levels of Participation

### 3.1 Ordinary Public, Nonpublic, or Unattributed Comments

Anyone may submit comments without identity verification. A GitHub username does not provide true anonymity; nonpublic comments, or comments whose authors do not want their account displayed in the body, may be submitted only after the nonpublic channel specified in the round announcement opens. Unverified comments may lead to revisions, but are counted only as unverified public comments or safely aggregated unverified nonpublic comments.

### 3.2 Formal Worker Review

Applicants for formal worker review should:

1. Provide at least one nonpublic means of contact;
2. Briefly describe their current or past industry, type of work, and experience;
3. Choose a low-risk way to substantiate that experience;
4. Consent to the information being used only for contact, category confirmation, deduplication, and review processing.

See [REVIEWER_RECRUITMENT_AND_SELECTION.en.md](REVIEWER_RECRUITMENT_AND_SELECTION.en.md) for the application process and selection rules, and use [WORKER_REVIEW_APPLICATION_FORM.en.md](WORKER_REVIEW_APPLICATION_FORM.en.md) for formal private applications. The public “Expression of Interest in Worker Review” Issue records only interest that may be made public; it is neither a formal application nor verification of worker eligibility. Do not include contact details, employers, precise locations, supporting documents, or real sensitive cases.

Applicants are not required to submit complete identity documents, employment contracts, payslips, bank cards, home addresses, internal company documents, or information about other people. Those who cannot or do not wish to undergo verification may still submit ordinary comments; refusing unnecessary documentation does not remove their opportunity to be heard.

---

## IV. What Workers Can Examine

- Whether they can understand provisions in their own words;
- Whether provisions reflect actual work experience and processes;
- Whether they identify exploitation, discrimination, unfairness, information asymmetry, and power imbalances;
- Whether they respect workers' rights to choose, appeal, negotiate, and participate in technology governance;
- Whether managers could use them to demand obedience, overtime, self-sacrifice, or acceptance of technology costs;
- Whether they overlook industries, roles, forms of employment, or particular risks;
- Whether they address wages, working hours, surveillance, performance assessment, layoffs, social insurance, safety, occupational diseases, algorithmic management, and technological displacement;
- Whether the language is too abstract, slogan-like, or requires specialist knowledge to use;
- Whether they can translate into AI behavior that is practically useful and transparent about risks.

---

## V. Structured Assessment

Scores are used only to identify problems. They do not evaluate workers themselves and are not added to expert scores.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Does not meet this dimension's objective at all |
| 1 | Has serious problems and needs major revision |
| 2 | Partly useful, but still needs substantial revision |
| 3 | Generally usable, with only minor revisions or additions needed |
| 4 | Very clear, grounded in reality, and provides adequate protection |
| N/A | Unrelated to my experience or impossible for me to assess |

Content that could harm workers is recorded separately with a “major risk” flag, which must not be conflated with a score of 0. A blank means unanswered; N/A means not applicable or unable to assess. Neither may be treated as 0.

Suggested dimensions:

1. Ease of understanding;
2. Consistency with actual work experience;
3. Coverage of key real-world issues;
4. Strengthening workers' agency and practical ability to act;
5. Identification of unequal power;
6. Prevention of misuse by managers against workers;
7. Fair treatment of technological benefits, costs, and risks.

Participants may answer only open-ended questions without assigning scores. Ability to express oneself, writing format, or use of theoretical terminology does not determine the value of a comment.

In its summaries, the project reports the number of valid scores, blanks, and N/A responses, the raw distribution, and the median for each provision and dimension. It does not use averages or rankings when scores are few or disagreement is substantial. Scores identify areas needing revision or investigation; they do not automatically determine acceptance, rejection, or “passing review.”

---

## VI. Eight Basic Questions

1. Can you understand this principle? What is hard to understand?
2. Does it reflect the work experience you know?
3. How might it help workers?
4. How might managers or other actors with power use it?
5. What real-world issues or people does it overlook?
6. Do you recommend keeping, revising, splitting, merging, or deleting it?
7. How would you express it more clearly?
8. Would you be willing to provide a sufficiently anonymized work situation?

---

## VII. Limits on the Use of Experience

- One person's experience may reveal a real problem but does not automatically represent an entire industry;
- The project should distinguish firsthand experience, secondhand accounts, speculation, and value judgments;
- Experience processed for safety may be used to identify leads concerning problems, provide a real-world basis for revising principles, design evaluation scenarios, test expert judgments, and reveal differences between industries and institutions; every use must respect participants' authorization and the limits of generalization;
- Before publishing a case, remove combinations of identifying details such as names, companies, precise locations, employee numbers, rare roles, and specific times;
- Participants are not required to submit evidence for rights claims to prove they “deserve to be heard”;
- Do not contact employers, platforms, supervisors, or coworkers for verification unless the participant separately gives explicit, fully informed consent;
- Where an ongoing labor dispute, risk of retaliation, or personal safety is involved, prioritize treating the information as a nonpublic risk lead rather than publishing it directly;
- When quoting or paraphrasing anonymized cases, the project should respect the scope of disclosure selected by the participant.

---

## VIII. Assisted Participation and Accessibility

Each formal review round must actually provide at least one low-bandwidth or assisted participation method independent of GitHub; formal review must not be announced as open without one. Possible methods include:

- Reviewing only one or a few principles;
- Responding through text interviews or orally;
- Having a trusted assistant record responses;
- Using translation or speech-to-text;
- Completing responses across multiple sessions rather than being required to finish at once;
- Asking the project to explain questions in plain language first.

Participants must confirm any transcribed, translated, or organized content. Assistants must not choose conclusions for participants, add experiences, or change their views. The project should disclose in its round summary which assistance methods were actually available and must not describe services not yet provided as already delivered.

---

## IX. Participant Selection and Representativeness

Targeted invitations should seek to cover different:

- Industries and roles;
- Regular, agency, temporary, platform, and flexible employment arrangements;
- Employment, unemployment, retirement, and occupational transition statuses;
- Regions and working environments;
- Types of exposure to algorithmic management, automation, surveillance, and safety risks.

The project must not select participants based on whether their views support the current draft. When participation is insufficient or certain groups are absent, it should disclose these gaps rather than claim overall consensus based on a small number of participants.

To prevent re-identification, small sensitive combinations should generally not be disclosed separately in public statistics; specific thresholds should be stated in the round announcement.

---

## X. Participation Burden and Compensation

Before recruitment for each round, the project should state the estimated time required, whether compensation is available, and its terms. The project currently makes no promises of remuneration, jobs, funding, equity, tokens, or future returns. If compensation is offered in the future, the same type of work within the same round should follow publicly stated, consistent standards; payment must not depend on the content of opinions or degree of agreement.

---

## XI. Safety, Withdrawal, and Feedback

- Do not submit information that could create a risk of retaliation using work devices, workplace networks, or environments visible to others;
- Do not disclose identifying information about actual workplaces, precise roles, supervisors, coworkers, clients, or family members;
- If private information is accidentally posted, report it immediately under [../../SECURITY.en.md](../../SECURITY.en.md), without adding details on public pages;
- Participants may request corrections, an end to contact, or deletion of nonpublic personal information;
- Unpublished comments may be withdrawn before the current round closes;
- Published content may be copied or forked. The project will address identification risks but cannot guarantee complete removal from all third-party copies;
- Formal worker submissions receive acknowledgment, reasoned feedback, and an opportunity for procedural reconsideration under [REVIEW_RESPONSE_POLICY.en.md](REVIEW_RESPONSE_POLICY.en.md).

Harsh criticism, low scores, refusal of attribution, or inability to use standard language are not grounds for invalidation. Impersonation, manipulation through duplicate submissions, harassment, fabricated experience, or deliberate exposure of others' private information may be marked invalid.
