# Privacy Notice for Review Participants

> **Chinese version:** [阅读中文版](PRIVACY_NOTICE.md)

> Status: Draft for public discussion<br>
> Version: v0.2-draft

This notice applies to applications, invitations, identity verification, formal reviews, and related communications involving expert and worker participation in the *Basic Principles for Workers' AI*. Ordinary public participation on GitHub is also affected by GitHub's platform rules and the nature of dissemination on the public internet.

## II. Essential Safety Reminders

- Do not submit contact details, identity documents, or proof of employment in GitHub Issues, Discussions, Pull Requests, or the code repository;
- A GitHub username is not an anonymous identity;
- Deleting a public page does not necessarily remove notification emails, caches, forks, clones, or history;
- Submit only the minimum information needed to complete the review;
- If an ongoing labor dispute, retaliation, or personal safety risk is involved, remove identifying information first and do not upload original evidence;
- If you accidentally disclose sensitive information, immediately report it through the security channel published in [../../SECURITY.en.md](../../SECURITY.en.md); do not continue explaining the matter publicly.

| Information category | Examples | Purpose | Public disclosure |

## IV. Information Not Collected as a Rule

- Complete front and back images of identity cards or other complete identity documents;
- Biometric information such as facial images, fingerprints, or voiceprints;
- Bank card details, financial accounts, or complete salary transaction records;
- Account passwords, access tokens, or security credentials;
- Precise home addresses;
- Medical, family, or private-life information unrelated to the review;
- Identity verification materials from participants under 18;
- Employers' trade secrets, confidential internal documents, or unauthorized communications;
- Materials that could identify colleagues, supervisors, clients, family members, or other third parties.

If a participant mistakenly submits unnecessary materials, the project should stop unnecessary processing, notify the participant to provide replacements, and delete the materials as soon as possible.

## V. Consent and Separate Authorizations

The project processes non-public information on the basis of participants' voluntary, explicit, fully informed choices. The following must not be bundled together:

- Participation in the current review round;
- Processing contact and verification information;
- Processing materials that may constitute sensitive personal information when genuinely necessary;
- Publishing a name or public designation;
- Publishing professional or work background;
- Publishing an employer or affiliation;
- Publishing the fact that the participant has completed formal verification for the expert or worker track;
- Publishing the full review text;
- Publishing a confirmed anonymized summary;
- Releasing public contributions under CC BY-SA 4.0;
- Continued contact for future rounds.

Participants should be informed again when processing purposes, methods, categories, retention periods, service providers, or disclosure scope change materially. Where the law requires renewed or separate consent, it must be obtained before processing continues.

Verification should prioritize public information, referrals, or real-time inspection without retention. Redacted employment contracts, salary records, social insurance records, and similar materials may still contain sensitive information. They should be considered only where the purpose is specific, the need is genuine, lower-risk methods are insufficient, and strict safeguards are in place; their necessity and potential impacts should be explained separately.

## VI. Data Separation and Access

Information is divided into three data domains:

1. Public review texts and registers;
2. Private contact details, authorizations, and identity mappings;
3. Temporary verification materials.

The three domains are initially linked through non-public, non-derivable random receipt identifiers. A separate set of public record identifiers is generated only after participants separately authorize publication and re-identification checks have been passed. Public repositories must not contain private receipt identifiers, identity mappings, or mappings between internal and public identifiers.

Minimum security requirements:

- Grant registered roles only the minimum necessary permissions;
- Separate identity verifiers from content decision-makers wherever possible;
- Enable multi-factor authentication where available from service providers;
- Log access, exports, changes, and deletions;
- Prohibit copying data to personal chats, public cloud drives, or other unapproved services;
- Review and revoke permissions when a role ends, a device is lost, or a round concludes;
- Include backups, recycle bins, and email attachments in deletion plans;
- At the end of each round, have the privacy lead record deletion completion dates and reasons for any outstanding deletions.

If available personnel or technology cannot meet these requirements, formal verification should stop, and only ordinary comments without identity materials should be accepted.

---

## VII. Retention Periods

The following are default maximum periods. Where the law requires otherwise or a security incident is being handled, retain only the necessary portions and record the reasons:

| Data | Default retention period |
| --- | --- |
| In-person or real-time verification content | As a rule, no audio or video recording and no retention of full conversations |
| Original verification attachments | Delete within 7 calendar days after verification is completed |
| Original verification attachments awaiting supplementary information | A maximum of 30 calendar days; delete if no response is received |
| Attachments involved in a verification reconsideration | Delete within 7 calendar days after reconsideration ends |
| Attachments genuinely needed for a special dispute | Document the necessity in writing; retain no later than 12 months after the round ends |
| Contact details | Delete within 90 calendar days after individual feedback is completed; for those consenting to future contact, reconfirm consent after no more than 12 months |
| Minimal verification status records | 12 months after publication of the round summary; then delete identity mappings and retain only anonymous aggregates |
| Unpublished review text | Delete or irreversibly anonymize within 12 months after publication of the round summary, unless separate authorization for archival retention is obtained |
| Access, authorization, and deletion records | 12 months after publication of the round summary |
| Content authorized for publication | Retained continuously as public version history, subject to public licensing and platform-copying limitations |

The actual participation announcement may set shorter periods. Periods cannot be arbitrarily extended without renewed notice.

## VIII. Participants' Choices and Requests

Participants may request:

- Confirmation of whether the project holds their non-public personal information;
- Copies of relevant information and authorization choices;
- Correction of incorrect contact details, classifications, or authorizations;
- Withdrawal of consent for subsequent processing based on consent;
- Deletion of information that is no longer necessary, has exceeded its retention period, or has been processed unlawfully;
- An end to contact for future rounds;
- An explanation of identity verification and invalidity determinations;
- Reconsideration of processing decisions;
- Reporting of privacy, security, and workplace retaliation risks.

The rights-request channel remains to be specified before participation opens. The project should verify the requester's connection to the relevant private receipt identifier, but must not require them to paste it on a public page or collect excessive identity information anew to respond to the request.

Withdrawal of consent does not affect compliant processing completed before withdrawal. Content already published and circulated under an open license may be retained by third parties. The project cannot guarantee deletion of every copy, but should address identifiable information still under its control and, where safety or legal risks arise, issue corrections, de-identify information, or cease further use.

---

## IX. Public Content and Anonymization

Before publication, check at least:

- Names, contact details, accounts, and identity documents;
- Employers, stores, work teams, precise locations, and uncommon roles;
- Specific dates, facts of disputes, and other clues that can identify someone in combination;
- Third-party information;
- Metadata, attachment filenames, image backgrounds, and document properties;
- Whether small-sample statistics could reveal individuals.

Where sensitive combinations in public statistics involve too few people, combine categories, broaden the time range, or withhold disclosure. If an anonymized summary could change the original meaning, ask reachable participants to confirm it before publication.

---

## X. Security Incidents

If unauthorized access, accidental publication, loss, or a risk of disclosure is discovered:

1. Immediately restrict access and suspend relevant submissions;
2. Do not spread sensitive details through public channels;
3. Retain only the minimum logs needed for investigation;
4. Assess the information, people, time periods, extent of copying, and real-world retaliation risks involved;
5. Take measures such as deletion, revoking permissions, rotating keys, and de-identification;
6. Promptly notify potentially affected participants and explain protective steps they can take;
7. Determine whether reporting to competent authorities is required under applicable law;
8. Publish an incident review and procedural improvements without exposing individuals.

Use the channel published in [../../SECURITY.en.md](../../SECURITY.en.md) for security issues.

---

## XI. Third-Party Services and Cross-Border Transfers

GitHub, email, web forms, cloud storage, and communication tools may be provided by third parties and may involve servers, recipients, or backups outside the country.

Before formally collecting non-public personal information, the project must disclose the service providers actually used, storage regions, processing purposes, data categories, retention arrangements, and routes for participants to exercise their rights, and complete the assessments, notices, and consent required by applicable law. Until this is complete, the technical ability to receive email must not be treated as adequate private verification capability.

Public GitHub contributions make content accessible worldwide. Participants should not include personal information in public contributions that they do not want disseminated globally.

---

## XII. Minors

At the current stage, formal identity verification is not conducted for people under 18, and their identity materials are not collected. If this becomes necessary in the future, dedicated rules must first be published and assessments of guardianship, necessity, and safety completed.

## XIV. Version Changes

Changes to processing purposes, disclosure scope, information categories, retention periods, service providers, or arrangements for providing information outside the country should record the differences, effective date, and applicable rounds.
