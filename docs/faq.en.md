# Frequently Asked Questions

> **Chinese version:** [阅读中文版](faq.md)

> Answers to common questions about the project. Raise an Issue if you have further questions.

---

**1. Does the project already have its own AI model?**
No. Repository content is marked v0.1.0; formal Git tag / Release status requires confirmation by the project lead. The project remains at the foundational stage, with principles, questions, a governance framework, and foundational-theory discussion drafts. A local open-source prototype will be explored only if conditions permit.

**2. Does the project think AI already has consciousness?**
No. It does not assume AI has consciousness, feelings, or autonomy, nor rule out some form of consciousness or autonomy in the future.

**3. Why use “working-class standpoint”?**
What AI learns from, who sets its objectives, whom it serves, and whose interests may be sacrificed are not merely technical questions. The project seeks a direction beyond capital-centered thinking, managerialism, and technical elitism, with workers participating in defining and evaluating AI.

**4. Is this just making AI recite Marxism–Leninism?**
No. Evaluation does not measure recitation of terminology. Fluent use of “class” or “capital” does not prove understanding of workers. Recognizing unequal power, respecting workers' rights, and giving truthful, practical advice may better reflect a working-class standpoint.

**5. What place does Marxism have in the project?**
The project values theoretical resources from Marxism, workers' movements, and other traditions of labor thought, while opposing mechanical dogmatism. Theory matters when it helps explain reality, change it, and advance human emancipation. Quoting classics does not by itself establish a standpoint.

**6. Can people who disagree with some of the theory participate?**
Yes. A shared theoretical background or agreement on every question is not required. Different views should be recorded and taken seriously.

**7. Can business managers and developers participate?**
Yes, while following open licenses, respecting workers' privacy, citing sources, and disclosing relevant business or institutional interests (see [../GOVERNANCE.en.md](../GOVERNANCE.en.md)). Participation must not be used to suppress workers' rights or monitor workers.

**8. How can people who do not program participate?**
You can [volunteer](volunteers.en.md) across project stages: contact workers, understand AI's impact and workers' hopes, spread the word, and help with future work. You can also write to the [workers' mailbox](../README.en.md#workers-mailbox). Other contributions include de-identified case leads, question reviews, factual corrections, theory papers, literature, translation, proofreading, testing AI responses, and helping others use GitHub. Email is available if you do not use GitHub. See [../CONTRIBUTING.en.md](../CONTRIBUTING.en.md).

**9. Does the project promise future returns?**
No. It does not issue tokens, redeemable points, shares, or certificates of return, or promise funding, jobs, computing resources, or economic returns. Contribution values are honorary recognition of “working for the working class,” with no material benefits. If a Workers' AI is trained and resources are sufficient, public discussion will determine how to give back, considering the AI's views and actual resources. No timing, form, or amount is set. See [the contribution honor roll](../CONTRIBUTORS.en.md).

**10. Does the project collect information from real workers?**
The project respects workers' right to submit real cases and decide what may be public. They may choose real-name, pseudonymous, publicly anonymous (without real names), or internal-only handling. Identities, workplaces, contact details, and originals are not published without explicit authorization. The project must not appropriate workers' experience without compensation in the name of Workers' AI. Email does not make senders anonymous to maintainers, who see their addresses. See [../SECURITY.en.md](../SECURITY.en.md) and [../CONTRIBUTING.en.md](../CONTRIBUTING.en.md).

**11. Does the project encourage hatred or violence?**
No. It opposes oppression based on ethnicity, gender, region, disability, or other identities, and prohibits personal attacks, harassment, threats, and hate speech. See [../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.en.md](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.en.md).

**12. Who maintains the project?**
The initiator has primary responsibility at the early stage. Stable contributors may become maintainers, with shared maintenance by workers, technical contributors, and content researchers explored over time.

**13. Why public evaluation?**
A working-class standpoint cannot be established by self-description alone. Public evaluation reveals value orientations, overlooked interests and risks, and managerial or technocratic influences. It should show distributions, major risks, and disagreements rather than only a leaderboard.

**14. How can the project avoid a new rule by technical elites?**
Technical contributions do not automatically grant permanent political or value authority. Core changes require discussion and review; governance should be open to workers, researchers, and ordinary participants. See [../GOVERNANCE.en.md](../GOVERNANCE.en.md).

**15. How can I report an error or propose a change?**
Use an Issue or Pull Request to explain what should change, why, and the factual basis. Those unable to use GitHub or whose theory texts are not ready for publication can email [worker.ai.spark@gmail.com](mailto:worker.ai.spark@gmail.com). Substantive core-file changes require public discussion and final review by the initiator or future maintainers.

**16. How can I submit a theory paper by email?**
Send it to [worker.ai.spark@gmail.com](mailto:worker.ai.spark@gmail.com), with subject `[Theory Submission] Title`. Include an abstract, attribution preference, sources and permissions, intended disclosure scope, and whether you consent to publication under CC BY-SA 4.0 after acceptance. The project will not copy an email manuscript into the public repository before explicit confirmation of final text, attribution, and licensing. Email is neither anonymous nor end-to-end encrypted; high-risk material should first be reduced to a minimal, de-identified overview. See [email submission guidance](../CONTRIBUTING.en.md#4-email-submissions).
