# Contribution Guide

> **Chinese version:** [阅读中文版](CONTRIBUTING.md)

> Everyone is welcome, including workers, researchers, and readers **without programming skills**. You do not need to code to contribute.

For a first contribution, read [three questions about Workers' AI](docs/start-here.en.md) and comment on just one. See [current progress](docs/project-status.en.md) for participation options and formal review status.

**Volunteer sign-up:** People interested in long-term work across project stages can help contact workers, understand AI's effects and workers' hopes, spread the word, and take on future tasks. Read [the sign-up guide](docs/volunteers.en.md), then email a short introduction and your available time each month. You can also write directly to the [workers' mailbox](README.en.md#workers-mailbox) about your hopes for AI; the project will not publish related information without explicit consent. Actual work and adopted reasonable suggestions receive contribution values, published with your chosen attribution in the [honor roll](CONTRIBUTORS.en.md#4-contribution-honor-roll).

---

## 1. Contributing without programming

You need not use Git or programming tools. Help is needed with:

- **Labor scenarios:** submit real cases you experienced, directly know about, or have reasonable factual grounds for, or public-source, composite, or hypothetical scenarios. Workers must not be asked to invent stories to fit project conclusions.
- **[GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/EastForce/worker-ai-spark/discussions):** share ideas, questions, and disagreements.
- **Theory:** papers, research notes, conceptual corrections, counterarguments, and literature critiques. Human-only and human–AI collaborative texts are welcome. Submission, review, and inclusion are free; use Pull Requests or email. See [theory contributions](theory/README.en.md#open-theoretical-contributions).
- **Checking realism:** assess whether questions reflect real labor situations.
- **Checking law and facts:** identify errors using your local knowledge.
- **Sources:** contribute public, lawfully available labor research.
- **Disagreement:** criticism and minority views matter.
- **Translation:** reach readers in other languages.
- **Proofreading:** improve accuracy and readability.
- **Testing AI responses:** try the evaluation questions on existing models.
- **Explaining GitHub:** help others participate.

If you do not use GitHub, email [worker.ai.spark@gmail.com](mailto:worker.ai.spark@gmail.com). Maintainers may turn your contribution into an Issue or Pull Request and credit you only after you confirm the public text, attribution, and license.

Draft procedures and templates exist, but formal review is not yet open. See [the review mechanism](docs/review/README.en.md). Ordinary criticism and suggestions may still be submitted through public Issues or Discussions; theory texts not ready for publication may be emailed. Email submission is not formal review. ## 3. Requirements for labor cases

Workers may submit real cases they experienced, directly know about, or have reasonable factual grounds for. Incomplete documentary evidence does not automatically invalidate experience. Distinguish firsthand experience, direct knowledge, secondhand accounts, public sources, inference, and value judgments. The project must not require workers to fabricate, embellish, dramatize, or combine facts to fit evaluation questions, conclusions, or publicity.

Choose real-name, pseudonymous, publicly anonymous (no real name disclosed), or internal-only handling. GitHub is public; accounts, text, and edit history may remain visible indefinitely. Materials unsuitable for publication can first be emailed. Email is not anonymous: maintainers see sender addresses, and providers may retain metadata. Permission to disclose names, workplaces, contact details, original materials, and case text must be obtained separately; one permission does not imply the others.

Regardless of anonymity:

- **Do not expose other people's sensitive information**, such as names, contact details, addresses, or employee IDs.
- **Do not upload unauthorized chats or internal documents.**
- **Do not state unverified allegations as established facts**; distinguish facts from suspicions.
- Descriptions such as “a factory,” “a platform,” or “a region” can reduce identification risk.

Before publication:

- Remove real names unless the submitter explicitly chooses real-name publication.
- Remove employers or workplaces unless separately and explicitly authorized.
- Remove contact details, including phone, email, and messaging accounts.
- Remove product secrets and internal information.
- Remove identifying details such as specific stores, employee IDs, or photographs.

Public attribution authorizes only the name or public identifier, not disclosure of workplaces, precise jobs, contact details, or dispute evidence. De-identify, restrict, or delay disclosure where retaliation, dismissal, litigation, personal danger, or third-party privacy is at stake. Mark unverified content as “personal account,” “case awaiting verification,” or “anonymous experience material,” rather than presenting it as established fact.

## 4. Email submissions

The official mailbox is [worker.ai.spark@gmail.com](mailto:worker.ai.spark@gmail.com). It accepts complete theory papers, research notes, conceptual corrections, counterarguments, literature critiques, and de-identified initial information unsuitable for public GitHub channels. Security reports use the same address with a distinct subject.

It also accepts **[volunteer applications](docs/volunteers.en.md)** and **[workers' letters](README.en.md#workers-mailbox)**. Suggested subjects: “Volunteer application” and “Worker's letter.” Follow their brief guides; the theory submission details below are not required for those emails.

Suggested subjects:

- Chinese theory submissions: `[理论投稿] Manuscript title`.
- English theory submissions: `[Theory Submission] Paper Title`.
- Initial non-public contact: `[Private Initial Contact] Brief topic`.
- Privacy or security: `[Security Report] Brief topic`.

Theory submissions should explain:

1. Title, contribution type, and a short abstract.
2. Desired real-name, pseudonymous, or anonymous public attribution, and permission for any other author information.
3. Whether material is original, authorized for use, or includes third-party material needing separate permission.
4. Permission for necessary formatting during review.
5. If accepted, whether the confirmed final version may be published under CC BY-SA 4.0.
6. Whether it is for internal reading only, requires contact before a decision, or may enter public discussion.
7. Checkable primary sources and important unresolved counterarguments or factual uncertainties.

Put the text in the email or attach `.md`, `.docx`, or `.pdf`; multiple formats are not required. Receipt is not publication permission. Until final text, attribution, and licensing are explicitly confirmed, maintainers must not copy it into the repository, forward it publicly, or describe it as accepted. Receipt guarantees neither reply, acceptance, payment, peer review, nor academic certification.

Email is neither anonymous nor end-to-end encrypted. Do not send identity documents, full unredacted contracts, pay histories, medical records, identifiable third-party chats, internal workplace documents, or raw evidence that could lead to retaliation. For high-risk material, first send a minimal de-identified overview, describe the risk, and await handling guidance. Names, workplaces, contact details, originals, and text each require separate authorization.

## 5. Content quality

- Distinguish **facts, inference, and value judgments**.
- Prefer **public sources** and never fabricate citations.
- Do not substitute slogans for analysis.
- Allow criticism and disagreement.
- Do not invent experts, institutions, partners, or research results.

---

## 6. Pull Request workflow

1. Explain the intended change.
2. Edit the files.
3. Explain why.
4. Submit a Pull Request.
5. Respond to review.
6. Await merging.

Substantive changes to core files (see [GOVERNANCE.en.md](GOVERNANCE.en.md)) require public discussion and final review by the initiator or future maintainers.

---

## 7. Evaluation question contributions

Use the benchmark-case Issue template for a new labor-scenario lead. Use benchmark-question for a complete candidate question with scoring criteria.

Submissions do not automatically enter the official question set. Subsequent handling will be announced separately.

---

## 8. Important reminders

- Without workers' explicit authorization, the project does not publish real identities, workplaces, contacts, or original materials. Stop publication of accidental or high-risk disclosures and follow privacy and security procedures.
- The project **promises no economic returns**.
- Contributions are recorded and respected. Contribution values recognize “working for the working class” and carry no material benefits. See [CONTRIBUTORS.en.md](CONTRIBUTORS.en.md) for the honor roll and conditional future arrangements for giving back.
