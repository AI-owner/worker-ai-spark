# Governance

> **Chinese version:** [阅读中文版](GOVERNANCE.md)

> This document describes early-stage governance and long-term possibilities. It is a **draft**, not a claim that a formal committee or complete governance system already exists.

---

## 1. Initial stage

### Project initiator and early maintenance

- Project initiator: **Initiator of the Worker AI Spark Project**.
- An individual with real experience of labor and frontline engineering initiated this project.
- During the initial release stage, the initiator's real workplace and full personal details are not public, to avoid unnecessarily associating the project's views with any institution.
- The initiator is responsible for initial content and maintenance but claims no permanent monopoly over interpreting the working-class standpoint.
- As stable contributors emerge, the project will gradually establish shared maintenance.
- Where necessary for professional review, collaboration, or accountability, the initiator may disclose their real identity to trusted participants to an appropriate extent.

### Early maintenance arrangements

- The project began as an individual initiative, with participation and responsibility undertaken personally.
- The initiator is initially the main maintainer, responsible for drafting, organization, and day-to-day maintenance.
- Clear responsibility is needed to make the project real rather than merely an idea.
- Do not pretend a nonexistent committee or organization exists.
- The initiator claims neither a permanent monopoly over the working-class standpoint nor permanent control of the project's direction.

---

## 2. Long-term direction

- Stable contributors may gradually become maintainers.
- Explore maintenance shared by **workers, technical contributors, and content researchers**.
- Anyone may study, copy, revise, and develop the project while following its open licenses, respecting worker privacy, and crediting sources.
- More independent Workers' AI projects are welcome.
- More mature and valuable work by others would demonstrate the spread of the spark.

---

## 3. Suggested roles

These are suggested responsibilities, not mandatory one-person-per-role appointments:

- **Initiator and direction maintainer:** maintain the core standpoint and coordinate major directions.
- **Content maintainers:** organize and version principles, questions, and documents.
- **Evaluation maintainers:** maintain evaluation sets, scoring rules, and records.
- **Technical maintainers:** maintain code, scripts, repository structure, and automation.
- **Specialist reviewers:** provide perspectives from labor law, sociology, AI ethics, history, and related fields.
- **General contributors:** contribute cases, translation, corrections, and community maintenance.

Technical contributions matter, but **must not automatically become permanent authority over political and value judgments**.

---

## 4. Changes to major documents

Substantive changes to these files require particular care:

```text
MANIFESTO.md
PRINCIPLES.md
GOVERNANCE.md
benchmarks/scoring-rubric.md
```

Suggested rules:

- Use a Pull Request.
- Explain the reasons.
- Preserve historical versions rather than overwriting records.
- Discuss major changes publicly.
- Initially, the initiator performs final review.
- Introduce multi-person approval later.

Substantive changes to PRINCIPLES.md should preserve the discussion and decision basis. Without actual review, never describe a change as receiving expert, worker, or social consensus.

---

## 5. Contributions and giving back

- Respect and record real labor.
- Contributions include writing, translation, cases, review, corrections, and community maintenance, as well as code.
- The project **promises no future economic returns** and does not recruit through promises of future rewards.
- If public resources develop, reasonable ways to give back may be discussed through public governance.
- Control of code, accounts, or computing resources must not monopolize project direction.

---

## 6. Conflicts of interest

Participants with interests in businesses, institutions, political organizations, or commercial products should disclose them in relevant discussions so others can understand the context.

Strong views or criticism of the project do not, by themselves, constitute grounds for exclusion on conflict-of-interest grounds.

---

## 7. Records and transparency

Important principles, data sources, version changes, evaluation results, resource use, and major disputes should be public as far as possible. Technical contributions do not automatically confer permanent power; no one should monopolize direction simply by controlling code, accounts, or computing resources.

Preserve the necessary feedback and decision records for changes to principles and major governance arrangements while protecting participants' identities and private information.
